1. Summary
Samajh is an Android app that a teacher uses to check how a child in Class 1 to 5 reads, what the child
understands and how the child writes. It then tells the teacher what to teach next. It addresses the
Learning-Level Visibility challenge: a speed score alone can rank a child who understood almost nothing
above a child who understood the story but decodes slowly.
This report does three things. It organises the team's 23 features into one flow that a teacher can follow. It
adds 10 features that strengthen the solution: 6 core features and 4 that only work with internet. And it
specifies how the app should look and behave so that it stays simple for a teacher who is checking children
in a busy classroom.

Design principles
Principle
What it means in the app
The teacher is the only user
The teacher operates the app. The child only reads, talks and writes. There are no
child-facing screens, accounts or games.
One screen, one job
The home screen has one main button. During a check, each screen shows one
instruction with one large button.
Offline first, better online
Reading is scored on the phone. Anything that needs the cloud is saved and finished
later, so the teacher never waits for a network.
Explained and correctable
Every result is shown in plain words, with the reason, and the teacher can correct a
marking before it is saved.
Every result ends in an
action
Each child gets a group, a next step and a practice sheet. The class gets a weekly plan.
No result is only a number.

2. How the app is organised
The app follows the teacher's real day: set up once, open the app, check a child, review, hand out practice,
and track progress. The six stages below are the same stages used to group the features in section 4.
1
Set up (once)
• Sign in with OTP
• Create class, add children
• Mark consent, try the guided demo
2
Today (home)
• See progress and to-do list
• Tap 'Check next child'
• Rechecks and waiting results shown
3
Check a child (~4 min)
• Read aloud, tell it back, write
• Voice guides every step
• Pause or resume at any step
4
Review
• Doubtful words highlighted
• Correct a marking if needed
• Save, open the next child
5
Plan and practice
• Group and next step shown
• Print the practice sheet
• Weekly plan for each group
6
Track and share
• Recheck after a few weeks
• Early warnings, progress
• Parent report; school view online
Navigation: four tabs only
Tab
Purpose
What is inside
Today
Home. What to do right
now.
Progress for the class, 'Check next child' button, short to-do list (rechecks due,
sheets ready, results waiting for network).
Class
Children and groups.
Roster with status of each child, four reading groups, common mistakes, early
warnings, weekly plan. Tapping a child opens the child result.
Practice
Material for tomorrow.
Practice sheets to print, weekly activities with tick-off, links to DIKSHA, NCERT
and state resources (online).
Reports
Progress and sharing.
Progress over time, parent reports and voice notes, school-level view for the
head teacher (online), PDF export.
Settings (language, PIN, backup, sync status) sit behind the profile icon at the top right, so they do not crowd the four tabs. The
head teacher is a teacher with one extra view inside Reports. It is the same app, not a second app.

Rules that keep the app simple
 Home has one main action. 'Check next child' is the largest element. The rest of the screen is a short
to-do list of at most three lines.
 Plain words first, numbers second. The result screen leads with the group name and one next step.
Speed, pause counts and confidence sit behind a 'details' tap.
 Same words everywhere. 'Group', 'practice sheet' and 'recheck' mean the same thing on every screen.
 Large targets, voice guidance. Buttons are large for use while standing, and the voice speaks each
instruction in the child's language.
 The network is never a blocker. If something needs internet, the app says 'Saved, will finish when
online' and moves on.
 Honest about doubt. When the app is unsure (noise, blurry photo), it says so and asks the teacher to
confirm instead of guessing.

6. Privacy and child safety
The app handles recordings and photos of young children, so privacy is part of the design and not an add-on.
These rules apply across all features.
Area
Rule
Consent
A child cannot be checked until a parent's consent is marked on the roster. The app records the
mark; how consent is collected follows each school's process.
Audio
Recorded and transcribed on the phone. Audio is not uploaded. Only the text of a retell, without
the child's name, is sent for scoring.
Photos
Names are blurred before upload. The photo is deleted once the words are read. Only the
word-level result is kept.
Storage
Results are encrypted on the phone, in transit and in the cloud.
Access
OTP sign-in plus a PIN. A teacher sees only their own classes. A head teacher sees summary
figures for the school, not individual audio or photos.
Sharing with
parents
Nothing is sent until the teacher approves it. A report contains only that child's own result and one
home activity.
Deletion
A teacher can delete a child's record, and it is removed from the backup too.
A legal review against India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, which has specific rules for children's data, should be
done before any real-school rollout.
