Problem and approachA speed score alone can rank a child who understood almost nothing above a child who understood the story but reads slowly. Teachers check reading by hand, rarely, and still have to guess what to teach. Samajh checks three things in one short session and keeps them apart:Reading aloud: accuracy and correct words per minute, marked word by word by the teacher from a recording.Understanding: typed questions (fact, word meaning, why and how, main idea) and an optional retell checklist.Writing: a short dictation from the words the child misread, marked by the teacher.Principles the app follows:The teacher is the only user. There are no child accounts, games, scores or rankings shown to children.Offline first. No feature needs the internet.Every result is explained in plain words and names its source. Nothing is invented: no benchmark, chapter or number without an official source.Every result ends in a teaching action.Privacy by design: audio and photos never leave the phone and are deleted after marking.3. The app picks a passage that fits the class and runs a 3-second room-noise check. The microphone permission is asked here, never in front of the child.The child sees a greeting in Hindi and reads the passage. The phone records locally. There is no timer, score or red mark on the child's screen.The child sees "शाबाश!" and hands back the phone. Only the teacher passcode leaves Student Mode.The teacher plays the recording and taps each word: correct, read differently, skipped, self-corrected, repeated, or not sure. "Not sure" never counts as an error. The recording is deleted when the marks are saved.The teacher ticks the story points the child retold, then asks the typed questions and marks each answer. Answers in the child's home language are accepted.Dictation: the teacher reads out 5 items chosen from the words the child misread. The teacher marks the notebook and may photograph it. The photo is encrypted and deleted on save.The result says in plain words what the child can do, shows reading and understanding separately, and compares them with the official target for that class and curriculum.4.2 Support groupsEach checked child is placed in one of four provisional groups. The teacher can move any child, with a note; the history is kept.GroupMeaningOn trackReads accurately and understandsUnderstands, needs help reading wordsUnderstands the story but decoding holds them backReads words, needs help understandingDecodes well but misses why and howNeeds help with bothNeeds support with reading and understanding. A listening check that shows understanding moves the child to the second group.4.3 Practice and reports (M7)Practice engine: turns each child's evidence into needs (matras, conjuncts, bindu, word reading, fluency, facts, why and how, word meanings, spelling, listening).Group plans: a 10-minute plan per group from an offline bank of 14 original activities in English and Hindi, plus a home activity.Decodable practice words: the child's misread words first, then words built only from aksharas the child has already read correctly.A4 worksheets: printed through Android's print screen. Hindi is drawn by Flutter's text engine because the PDF library misprints Hindi (it printed "कतिाब" instead of "किताब").Child report: results, gaps, recommended action, practice words, suggested recheck date, and progress only between comparable checks.Parent report: plain Hindi or English, no scores or rankings. It can only be printed or shared after the teacher ticks an approval box.Class dashboard: checked and waiting counts, group counts, most often misread aksharas, spelling issues, rechecks due, and changes between comparable checks. Empty states instead of made-up numbers.4.4 Screenshots from the appReal captures from the app running on a 2 GB RAM Android emulator. Known limitationsThe reading timer starts when the passage appears, so a very early "done" tap gives an unrealistic words-per-minute figure.All 9 passages and 14 activities are original and still need review by a teacher.The scoring rubric (v0.1) is provisional and needs calibration against teachers' own marking in a pilot.There is no speech recognition and no handwriting recognition. The teacher marks reading and writing; the app does the analysis.Typing what a child wrote needs a Hindi keyboard on the teacher's phone.Uttar Pradesh textbook titles for Classes 1, 2, 4 and 5 are not verified (the official site was down).Marathi is not included yet.The test APK is signed with a debug key and is for testing only, not for the Play Store.9.
