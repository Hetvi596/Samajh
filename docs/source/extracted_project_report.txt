1. Summary
Samajh lets one teacher, on one Android phone, run a four-minute reading check with a child and see reading and understanding as separate results, then get a practice plan. Six of ten milestones are complete and tested, the seventh is built, and a test APK is ready. All data so far is a fictional demo class: real children's data stays blocked until the privacy checklist and a legal review are done.

2. Problem and approach
A speed score alone can rank a child who understood almost nothing above a child who understood the story but reads slowly. Teachers check reading by hand, rarely, and still have to guess what to teach. Samajh checks three things in one short session and keeps them apart:
Reading aloud: accuracy and correct words per minute, marked word by word by the teacher from a recording.
Understanding: typed questions (fact, word meaning, why and how, main idea) and an optional retell checklist.
Word check: child-facing visual choice items (writing position on ruled notebook lines, spelling, sound recognition, meaning) completed by the child in Student Mode without teacher marking.
Principles the app follows:
The teacher is the only user. There are no child accounts, games, scores or rankings shown to children.
Offline first. Core check flows and reports work completely without the internet; optional cloud backup is off by default.
Every result is explained in plain words and names its source. Nothing is invented: no benchmark, chapter or number without an official source.
Every result ends in a teaching action.
Privacy by design: audio recordings are ephemeral and deleted after marking; zero photos, camera permissions, or OCR.

4.1 A check, step by step
The teacher opens a child and taps Start a check. The app picks a passage that fits the class and runs a 3-second room-noise check. The microphone permission is asked here, never in front of the child.
The child sees a greeting in Hindi and reads the passage. The phone records locally. There is no timer, score or red mark on the child's screen.
Immediately following the passage, the child enters Word check: 4 large shuffled visual choices per item with audio cues and 'Not sure' neutral skip. Writing-position items render Devanagari words on notebook ruled lines using CustomPainter (hanging from line, floating, sinking, crossing).
The child sees "शाबाश!" and hands back the phone. Only the teacher passcode leaves Student Mode.
The teacher plays the recording and taps each word: correct, read differently, skipped, self-corrected, repeated, or not sure. "Not sure" never counts as an error. The recording is deleted when the marks are saved.
The teacher ticks the story points the child retold, then asks the comprehension questions and marks each answer. Answers in the child's home language or accepted regional dialect are accepted fully without penalty.
Word check results (teacher view only): displays raw count and weighted score (first-try 1.0, wrong-to-right 0.75, right-to-wrong 0, 3+ changes x0.5, slow x0.85 with 0.5 floor, fast tap guess flag), along with top 3 mistake tags.
The result says in plain words what the child can do, shows reading and understanding separately, and compares them with the official target for that class and curriculum.

4.3 Practice and reports (M7)
Practice engine: turns each child's evidence into needs (matras, conjuncts, bindu, word reading, fluency, facts, why and how, word meanings, spelling, listening).
Group plans: a 10-minute plan per group from an offline bank of 14 original activities in English and Hindi, plus a home activity.
Decodable practice words: the child's misread words first, then words built only from aksharas the child has already read correctly.
A4 worksheets: printed through Android's print screen. Hindi is drawn by Flutter's text engine because the PDF library misprints Hindi (it printed "कतिाब" instead of "किताब").
Child report: results, gaps, recommended action, practice words, suggested recheck date, and progress only between comparable checks.
Parent report: plain Hindi or English, no scores or rankings. It can only be printed or shared after the teacher ticks an approval box.
Class dashboard: checked and waiting counts, group counts, most often misread aksharas, spelling issues, rechecks due, and changes between comparable checks. Empty states instead of made-up numbers.

8. Known limitations
The reading timer starts when the passage appears, so a very early "done" tap gives an unrealistic words-per-minute figure (guarded by evidence honesty threshold).
All 9 passages and 14 activities are original and still need review by a teacher.
The scoring rubric and Word check weights (v0.2) are provisional and marked as awaiting calibration against teachers' own marking in a pilot.
Word check item bank (48 items) and regional pronunciation variants are provisional and need linguist and teacher verification.
There is no automated speech or pronunciation recognition. The app does not detect pronunciation automatically; the teacher listens and marks reading words. Words default to unmarked until explicitly tapped or confirmed as correct by the teacher. The child selects visual items; the teacher marks oral reading.
Uttar Pradesh textbook titles for Classes 1, 2, 4 and 5 are not verified (the official site was down).
Marathi is not included yet (prepared as drop-in pack).
The test APK is signed with a debug key and is for testing only, not for the Play Store.

11. Installing the test APK
Copy Samajh-v0.1.0-test.apk to an Android phone (Android 8 or later).
Open it and allow installing from this source when Android asks.
Open Samajh, choose English or Hindi, read the privacy promise and set a 6-digit passcode.
The app opens a demo class "3B" with 10 fictional children. Try: Class tab → a child → Start a check; the Class 3 milestones page; the Practice and Reports tabs.
This build is for testing only. It contains no real children's data and sends nothing over the internet.
